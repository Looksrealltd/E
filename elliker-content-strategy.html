Brand Profile

Aesthetic & Creative Identity

Visual Tone
Editorial nature photography. Moody, textural, unhurried. Raw landscapes and slow detail. Weathered stone, rippling water, mist over moorland.
Colour World
Earthy neutrals (sand, khaki, taupe) with deliberate pops of lime and steel blue. Nature's palette — no neons, no pastels. Grounded and confident.
Typography Feel
Clean, minimal sans on product. Prose-led storytelling in editorial contexts. "Field Guide" language — instructional, curious, lyrical.
Origin Story
Named after Elliker Fields & Woodland, East Keswick, Yorkshire. Place-rooted. The brand is inseparable from landscape.
Content Universe
Forest bathing, wild garlic foraging, rockpooling, mushroom ID, bird watching, hammock camping, charcoal burning, microscopy. Curious, cross-disciplinary outdoors.
Brand Tension (Useful)
Technical performance gear that looks like art. Recycled plastic bottles that become backpacks. Urban carry meets countryside escape. Price point accessible but values premium.
Phase 01

Customer Questions

30 likely customer questions generated from website intelligence only. No external sources were provided, so these are derived exclusively from verified website content — product range, sustainability pages, brand story, and projects. Clustered into 5 strategic themes.

A — Sustainability Credibility (the sceptic's cluster)
  • Is the "recycled plastic" story actually meaningful, or greenwashing?
  • What does GRS certification actually mean for my bag?
  • Does "100% recycled polyester" affect how the bag performs in rain?
  • How many plastic bottles go into making one backpack?
  • Does donating 1% of profits actually make a difference to wildlife?
  • Are the dyes and hardware also sustainable, or just the fabric?
B — Product Performance & Durability (the practical buyer)
  • How waterproof is "waterproof coating" — can I use it in heavy rain?
  • Will the roll-top seal properly with a full load?
  • How does a 24L expand to 35L on the Buckden backpack?
  • Is the sling bag big enough for a day hike vs just commuting?
  • What's the difference between a sacoche and a crossbody?
  • How does the softshell jacket perform vs a hardshell in real conditions?
C — Style & Identity (the fashion-meets-function buyer)
  • Is Elliker streetwear, outdoors gear, or something in between?
  • Does the lime colour look as good in person as online?
  • Will the bag look wrong if I'm not actually hiking?
  • How does Elliker compare aesthetically to brands like Cotopaxi or Fjällräven?
  • Are the products unisex? How do they fit across different body types?
  • Does the grid fleece hoodie work as a standalone or just a mid-layer?
D — Brand Story & Connection (the values-driven buyer)
  • Where exactly is Elliker Fields? Can I visit it?
  • Which specific UK Wildlife Trusts receive the 1% donation?
  • What is forest bathing and why does a bag brand care about it?
  • What is the Hikerdelic collaboration and who is Hikerdelic?
  • How do I actually do the activities featured in your "projects" content?
  • Is this a Yorkshire brand? Does that affect how I feel about buying it?
E — Purchase Friction & Trust (the almost-buyer)
  • Why should I pay £75 for a backpack when I can get one cheaper on Amazon?
  • Where can I try the gear in person before buying? (Stockists?)
  • What's the returns policy if the fit isn't right?
  • Do you ship internationally and how long does it take?
  • Is the brand growing — will you still be around to honour a warranty?
  • What do real customers say about the quality after 6 months of use?

Phase 03

Creative Concepts

Five high-retention video concepts built on unexpected contrast, visual tension, and satisfying resolution. Each is grounded in verified brand claims only.

Concept 01
Your Bag Used to Be a Bottle
  • Hook Close-up of plastic bottles piling up in a landfill. Then a hand reaches into the pile — pulls out one bottle. Time-lapse dissolve. Same hand now holds the Buckden backpack.
  • Contrast The material is trash. The object is beautiful. The gap between those two facts is the story.
  • Payoff Text: "We turned post-consumer plastic waste into this." Bag drops onto heather moorland. Sits perfectly.
  • Analogy Like turning old newspaper into a beautiful book — the past doesn't limit what the future can become.
  • Brand link 100% recycled polyester bags, GRS certified. [WEBSITE]
Concept 02
The Bag That Belongs in Both Worlds
  • Hook Split-screen, no music. Left: office corridor, fluorescent light, hard floors, briefcase crowd. Right: boggy moorland trail, wind noise, sheep blur past. Same bag visible in both frames.
  • Contrast Outdoor gear usually looks embarrassing on city streets. City bags look ridiculous on a hillside. This exists in the gap between those two worlds.
  • Payoff Screens merge into one. Person walking out of an office building, immediately into a park, pack on. Tagline: "Getting Into Nature — wherever that starts."
  • Analogy Like how a good Swiss Army knife looks right at the dinner table and on a campsite — it doesn't apologise for being both.
  • Brand link "Lightweight, functional" + aesthetic colour palette. [WEBSITE]
Concept 03
The Field That Started Everything
  • Hook Drone shot descending slowly over woodland in East Keswick. No branding. Just sound. Someone walking a path below. Then text appears: "This is where it started."
  • Contrast Most brands start in boardrooms with spreadsheets. This brand started in a specific field in Yorkshire. The contrast is location as origin — raw, unglamorous, real.
  • Payoff Cut to: Elliker logo. "We named ourselves after a field. Because we think where you come from should shape everything you make." Donate 1% of profits to UK Wildlife Trusts.
  • Analogy Like a chef named after their grandmother's kitchen — the name carries the whole story before you even taste the food.
  • Brand link Named after Elliker Fields, East Keswick. Donates to East Keswick Wildlife Trust. [WEBSITE]
Concept 04
Forest Bathing in a Bag
  • Hook Someone in a lab coat measuring "stress hormones" in a vial. Cut to: same person barefoot under trees. Cut to: the word "Shinrin-yoku" appearing in text over fog.
  • Contrast The confusion: is this a wellness brand? A science video? A Japanese practice piece? Then the reveal: "We make the gear. You make the medicine."
  • Payoff The bag being packed for a forest walk. Simple. Unhurried. Text: "Getting Into Nature is the oldest health hack. We just make sure you bring the right bag."
  • Analogy Like a good pair of running shoes — they don't make you a runner, but bad shoes will definitely stop you running.
  • Brand link Forest Bathing Project featured on website. Lightweight, functional products. [WEBSITE]
Concept 05
What Gets Certified vs What Gets Talked About
  • Hook Hands flipping through paper certificates — official-looking, stamped, dense text. Then: someone dumping a bag in a stream crossing. Splashing through. Bag emerges. Still intact. Soaking but closed.
  • Contrast Certifications (GRS, OCS 100) are the behind-the-scenes proof. The performance is the visible proof. Most brands choose one to talk about. This concept runs them side by side.
  • Payoff "The GRS certificate is for the planet. The waterproof coating is for the weather. Both matter. Both exist." Product close-up showing both fabric and water beading off surface.
  • Analogy Like a restaurant with a health certificate in the window and a full house — the official bit earns trust, the full house proves it.
  • Brand link GRS certificates, waterproof coating on bags, "minimal impact / maximum durability." [WEBSITE]

⟶ Pause point: Review the 5 concepts above. Reply with which you want to develop further, combine, or remove before the 30-day plan is built around them.
